Writing a Strong CV

Your CV is your first impression — make it count.

1

Keep it to 2 pages

Employers spend seconds scanning CVs. Be concise and only include what is relevant.

2

Start with contact details

Name, phone number, email and city. Do not include your full home address.

3

Write a personal profile

3–4 sentences about who you are, what you offer and what you are looking for.

4

List education in reverse order

Most recent qualification first. Include the institution, qualification and year completed.

5

Include all experience

Volunteer work, community projects, school leadership and part-time jobs all count.

6

Use strong action verbs

"Managed", "Created", "Assisted", "Organised", "Led" — start each bullet with a verb.

7

Proofread carefully

Spelling and grammar errors create a poor first impression. Ask someone else to read it too.

8

Save and send as PDF

PDF preserves your formatting across all devices and operating systems.

9

Tailor it for each application

Adjust your personal profile and skills to match the specific opportunity you are applying for.

Writing a Cover Letter

A strong cover letter sets you apart from other applicants.

Address it correctlyUse "Dear [Name]" if you know it, or "Dear Hiring Manager" if you do not.
Paragraph 1 — The openingState the position you are applying for and where you found it.
Paragraph 2 — Why you are a good fitLink your specific skills and experience to the requirements of the role.
Paragraph 3 — Why this organisationShow genuine enthusiasm for the specific organisation. Research them first.
Paragraph 4 — The closeThank them for their time and state that you look forward to hearing from them.
Keep it to one pageNo more than 4 short paragraphs. Hiring managers read hundreds of letters.
Customise every letterGeneric letters are easy to spot. Always tailor your letter to the specific role and organisation.

Preparing for Interviews

Preparation is the difference between a good interview and a great one.

1

Research the organisation

Know what they do, their mission, values and any recent news before you walk in.

2

Prepare for common questions

"Tell me about yourself", "Why do you want this role?", "What are your strengths and weaknesses?"

3

Dress appropriately

Smart casual or formal depending on the organisation. When in doubt, dress more formally.

4

Arrive 10–15 minutes early

Plan your route the day before. Know exactly where you are going and how long it takes.

5

Bring your documents

Bring copies of your CV, ID, certificates and any other documents requested.

6

Send a thank-you email

Within 24 hours of the interview, send a brief thank-you email to the interviewer.

The STAR Method

Use this framework to answer behavioural interview questions like "Tell me about a time when…"

S

Situation

Describe the context and background of the situation you were in.

T

Task

Explain the task or challenge you were responsible for.

A

Action

Describe the specific actions you took to address the task.

R

Result

Share the outcome. What did you achieve? What did you learn?

Creating a Professional Online Profile

Many South African employers search LinkedIn before they even advertise a role.

Set up a LinkedIn profileGo to linkedin.com and create a free account. Use your real name and a professional email address.
Use a professional photoClear, well-lit, neutral background. Dress as you would for an interview. Smile.
Write a compelling headlineE.g. "Aspiring Software Developer | Matric Graduate | Cape Town" — be specific and searchable.
Complete your About sectionWrite 3–5 sentences about your background, skills and career goals. Similar to your CV personal profile.
List all education and experienceInclude volunteer work, school leadership roles, community projects and any part-time work.
Add skills and get endorsementsList relevant skills and ask teachers, mentors or peers to endorse you for them.
Also register on SA Youth MobiGo to sayouth.mobi — a free government-supported platform that matches you directly with employers.

Preparing Your Application Documents

Having the right documents ready saves time and avoids missed deadlines.

1

Get certified copies

Most applications require certified copies of your ID and certificates. Visit your nearest police station or commissioner of oaths — it is free.

2

Proof of residence

A utility bill, bank statement or affidavit not older than 3 months. If you do not have a bill, a signed affidavit from a police station is accepted.

3

Academic transcripts

Available from your school, TVET college or university. Request them early — they can take time to process.

4

Reference letters

From teachers, community leaders, church leaders or previous employers. Ask at least 2 weeks in advance.

5

Scan documents clearly

Use a free app like Adobe Scan or Microsoft Lens. Make sure all text is readable and pages are not cut off.

6

Name your files clearly

Use a format like "Firstname_Surname_ID.pdf" so employers can identify your documents easily.

Identifying Employment Scams

Protect yourself — scammers target young job seekers. Know the warning signs.

Legitimate employers, learnerships and bursaries will never ask you to pay a fee to apply. If someone asks for money, it is a scam.

They ask for payment

Any "registration fee", "admin fee" or "training deposit" is a red flag. Walk away immediately.

Unsolicited job offers

If you receive a job offer you never applied for, be very suspicious — especially via WhatsApp or SMS.

Gmail or Yahoo email addresses

Legitimate companies use their own domain (e.g. @capaciti.org.za), not free email services.

Poor spelling and grammar

Many scam messages contain obvious errors and unprofessional language. Legitimate organisations proofread their communications.

Requests for banking details

No legitimate employer needs your bank account number before you are hired and have signed a contract.

Too good to be true

Extremely high salaries for entry-level roles with no experience required are almost always scams.

Pressure to decide quickly

Scammers create urgency. Legitimate employers give you reasonable time to consider an offer.

Cannot verify the company

Always search for the company independently. If you cannot find an official website or CIPC registration, be very cautious.
